  - q: Is a founder's office the same as a chief of staff?
    a: No. A chief of staff is primarily a coordination role, managing the founder's attention and information flow across an organisation. A founder's office is the person a founder hands a half-formed idea to and trusts that it comes back as a working business. Most people hear the term and picture someone taking notes in board meetings. In practice, across four ventures and four countries, it's looked more like: setting up a US entity from a laptop in India, negotiating a hostel-style guesthouse deal in the Maldives, or building the spreadsheet that finally told a founder where their money was actually going.

The job title changes — Head of International Operations, Events & Experience Curator, Founder's Office Associate — but the function is the same.

## The three things that never change

Three properties held constant across every version of this job, and they define the role more reliably than any job title.

1. **You're closest to the founder's actual priorities**, not the org chart's version of them.
2. **You operate with minimal oversight**, especially in 0-to-1 environments where there's no playbook yet.
3. **You're expected to close the loop** — not just plan the GTM launch, but watch it through to the first customer.

That last one is the part people underestimate. Anyone can build a plan. Founders don't need more plans. They need someone who'll still be there when the plan meets a customs form, a visa delay, or a vendor who ghosts on delivery day.

## Why cross-border makes it harder — and more useful

Cross-border versions of this role are harder because they add a regulatory surface most operators never touch, and more useful for exactly the same reason. Every one of my roles has involved at least two countries. That means compliance headaches (ODI filings, multi-currency billing, work-visa navigation) that most operators never touch. But it also means founders lean on you harder, because you're solving problems they can't see from where they're sitting.

If you're building a founder's office function into your own company, my one piece of advice: hire for judgment under ambiguity before you hire for domain expertise. The domain, you can teach. The judgment either survived a 0-to-1 environment already, or it hasn't been tested yet.
