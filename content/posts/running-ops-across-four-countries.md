By the time I was building the India–US operating bridge for an AI/robotics startup, I'd already run hospitality and investment operations in the Maldives and closed angel rounds for two space-tech ventures. None of it came with a manual. Here's what actually held it together.

## 1. Compliance first, growth second

It's tempting to chase the exciting part — the first US customer, the big tourism booking, the investor deck. But ODI filings, entity setup, and work-visa compliance are the load-bearing walls. Get them wrong and everything built on top of them is at risk. I've watched founders want to skip this step to move faster. It never actually saves time.

## 2. One source of truth for money, in one currency you trust

Managing a ~₹1 Cr monthly budget across currencies taught me this the hard way: if your billing and reporting live in five spreadsheets and three currencies, nobody — including the founder — actually knows the real number. Centralizing cross-border billing into one dashboard was the single highest-leverage thing I built in that role.

## 3. The team doesn't need you to have the answer — they need you to close the loop

Coordinating a 17-person multinational team in the Maldives, or a 6-person cross-border team for a robotics startup, taught me that most operational chaos isn't a knowledge problem. It's a follow-up problem. People start things. Someone has to make sure they finish.

## What I'd tell someone starting this kind of role tomorrow

Get comfortable being the least specialized person in the room and the only person who knows how all the pieces fit together. That's the job.
